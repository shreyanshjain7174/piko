the good bit

Dictation types what you said.
Piko types what you meant.

Your phone can already turn sound into words. It still can't write the way you write. Piko keeps a private little model of your phrasing, and every time you fix something, it learns — right there on the device.

three tricks

One button. Piko figures out the rest.

A small language model on the phone reads what you meant and picks the job for you. Nothing to switch, nothing to remember.

"tell them Thursday works"

Write it

Lands as text in whatever field you're standing in. Messages, mail, code comments, a search box, some random form. Anywhere the keyboard goes.

"remind me to pay the invoice"

Do it

Reminders, calendar, notes, timers, your own connected tools. Piko reads the intent and runs it — no confirmation screen for the obvious stuff.

"what did I say about pricing?"

Remember it

Every session stays searchable on your phone. Months of talking, findable in a second, stored nowhere else.

the promise

Piko has no way to phone home.

Not "encrypted in transit." Not "deleted after 30 days." There is no server, no account and nowhere to send anything. Your voice becomes text on your own phone's chip and goes to exactly one place: the field you were already typing in.

Flip on airplane mode and nothing changes. Easiest thing in the world to check, and you should.

Hearing
Apple's on-device speech stack — clocked at 2.2× faster than the quickest local Whisper build on a 34-minute file.
Tidying
A small language model living on the phone. Apple's built-in one, or your own 3–4B model at 4-bit if you're that kind of person.
Memory
A local index with retrieval, so months of sessions fit through an 8,192-token window.
Looks
Skins are local too. Piko's face, colours and voice live on the device with everything else.
Cost
No inference bill means no subscription for the core loop. Talk as much as you like.
optional friend

Piko Pebble.

A little clicker for your desk or pocket that wakes Piko without touching the phone. It talks to Piko directly instead of pretending to be a Bluetooth keyboard, so nothing else on your phone ever sees the click.

  • ClickStart talking. Click again to stop.
  • HoldChange voice — work, personal, code.
  • DoubleScratch that. Undo the last drop.
  • BuzzA little haptic back, so you never look down.

Only gets made if the app earns it. A button for an app nobody opens is a keyring.

being honest

What Piko can't do.

iOS puts real walls around every app. Better you read them here than find out on day two.

can do
  • Types into any app with a normal text field
  • Works with the network switched off entirely
  • Learns your words, names and phrasing from your fixes
  • Keeps every session searchable on the phone
  • Wakes from Back Tap, Action Button, Control Center, Siri or the Pebble
can't do
  • Password and phone-number fields — iOS swaps out every third-party keyboard there
  • Press buttons inside other apps. No iOS app is allowed to
  • Listen in the background. The mic opens when you open it, never before
  • Run on iPhones too old to hold the model in memory
  • Ask for an account before it's done anything useful
